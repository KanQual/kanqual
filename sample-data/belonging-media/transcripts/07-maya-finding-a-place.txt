Maya | Fictional 20-year-old second-year first-generation student

This is a fictional student diary for the Kanqual demonstration. The voice is synthesized.

I'm Maya. I'm in my second year at Alderbridge. If you had asked me last fall whether I belonged here, I probably would have said yes, because I thought that was the answer I was supposed to give. I had the scholarship and the campus sweatshirt. But I ate lunch looking at my phone so nobody would notice I was sitting alone.

Last week I went to A Seat at the Table, the first-generation lunch. The poster said, come on your own. That sounds small, but it mattered. Usually I assume everyone will arrive with friends. I stood outside Room 104 for a minute, and a student at the door asked whether I wanted to sit with her. She didn't ask me to introduce myself to the whole room.

We talked about office hours. I admitted I used to think you could only go if you were failing. Two other people laughed because they had thought the same thing. It was the first time that not knowing an unwritten rule didn't feel like evidence that admissions had made a mistake.

I saw one of them in the library yesterday. They moved their bag and made space for me before I even asked. That's what belonging feels like to me now. Somebody expects you to be there. You're not starting from zero every time you walk into a room.

It isn't everything. There's a Founders' Evening coming up, and forty-five dollars is my grocery money. I don't own formal clothes, either. I can feel included at lunch and still feel that another part of the university isn't meant for me. Both things are true.

I'm going to the garden welcome day on Sunday. I don't know anything about gardening. The flier says tools are provided, which takes away one thing to worry about. Maybe I'll ask the person from the library to come. I think that's the change. I'm starting to invite people instead of waiting to be invited.
