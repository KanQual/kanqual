Eli | Fictional 23-year-old third-year commuter with paid work and family responsibilities

This is a fictional student diary for the Kanqual demonstration. The voice is synthesized.

I'm Eli, a third-year student. I commute about forty minutes, and I work at a grocery store four evenings a week. I like my classes. I want to be clear about that. My seminar professor knows my name, and our group project is going well. But being comfortable in a classroom isn't the same as feeling part of the university.

I saw the Stories After Hours poster. Poetry and music, free entry. It sounded like exactly the kind of thing I'd enjoy. Then I looked at the time. Eight until ten at night. My last practical bus connection leaves before it's over, and on Fridays I'm usually closing at work anyway. Free doesn't mean I can get there.

People say, you should get involved. They mean it kindly, mostly. But it starts to sound as though being left out is something I'm choosing. I can't keep explaining my shift schedule every time someone asks why I wasn't at an event.

Yesterday I tried the Between Classes lunch. I only had twenty minutes. Nobody made it strange when I left early. Another commuter told me there's a group chat where people arrange study sessions before their buses. I joined it. So I don't want to say nothing works. That lunch worked because somebody had thought about when we'd actually be here.

The university promotion makes campus look like one big shared living room. I watch that and wonder where the people who leave at three fit into the picture. I don't need to be in an advertisement. I need a place to sit without buying coffee, and plans that don't always start after dinner.

There's a listening session called Make Room next month. I'll be working, but the flier says written comments are welcome. I might leave one. I want to know what happens after the comments, though. If they post a summary and change even one event time, I'll notice. Belonging would mean I don't have to rearrange my whole life just to have a small part in campus life.
